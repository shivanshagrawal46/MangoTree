In one line: our system's sold price equals the State's official record to the dollar on both houses, because both come from the recorded deed. The number Redfin shows as the house's current value is a model, and today it is 11% and 49% above what buyers actually paid. The ARV engine prices from the sold figure and never from the estimate or the asking price.

Why HouseCanary and our system are more accurate than Redfin and Zillow

  1. Our number is the deed; their headline number is a model. RealEstateAPI and HouseCanary deliver the recorded sale from the county record, the same record SDAT publishes. On both houses the three agree to the dollar. Redfin and Zillow carry the sold price too, but only after a listing closes, and what they lead with is the Redfin Estimate or the Zestimate. On Woodbrook that estimate is $230,819 against a sale at $155,000.
  2. The portal estimates are built on the wrong comps. Redfin's estimate for Woodbrook rests on six MLS sales: $20,000, $55,000, $65,000, $90,000, $315,000 and $317,000. Three shells, one as-is house and two luxury rehabs, averaged by a formula that cannot see condition. Fulton's estimate is built the same way, from $20,000 to $317,000. Our engine never averages like that. The analyst reads each sale's deed chain and financing, keeps five comparable finished houses, and sets aside shells, auctions and LLC transfers with a written reason.
  3. We know whether a sale was a market sale. The deed record carries the conveyance type. Both houses have a prior investor purchase ($80,000 and $95,000) that SDAT codes as non-arm's-length. Our engine rejects those as comps. A portal shows every transfer as "sold", or not at all. The $20,000 shell on Francis St is a "sold" row on Redfin and a comp in its estimate; in our grid it is set aside.
  4. We see who bought it and how. A 98% purchase mortgage from Truist plus a City of Baltimore assistance loan (Woodbrook), or a 98% loan from United Wholesale Mortgage (Fulton), means a family bought a finished house. An LLC paying cash means an investor bought a project. That is the difference between an after-repair comp and an as-is comp, and it is on the mortgage record, which neither portal shows.
  5. Portals miss the sales that did not go through the MLS. Redfin and Zillow are built on the Bright MLS feed. Off-market, wholesale and investor-to-investor sales, a large share of Baltimore transactions, never appear as listings and show only as thin public-record rows, if at all. Both investor purchases above were off-market. The deed record has every one of them.
  6. We grade condition from evidence, and show our working. HouseCanary gives a C1 to C6 grade plus the county's condition word, and the engine shows both next to the deed chain and the listing photos. Where the grade looks stale against the evidence, the analyst's reading of the sale wins and the disagreement is marked. Redfin and Zillow show listing photos, which are useful, but no grade and nothing at all for a house that never listed.
  7. Every number is traceable. Each price in the ARV file carries its source, its record date and the deed reference (Liber and Folio). Anyone can open the SDAT page and see the same figure. A Zestimate or a Redfin Estimate cannot be traced to anything.
  8. Two feeds catch each other's mistakes. HouseCanary and RealEstateAPI both label the investors' off-market purchases as arm's-length; SDAT does not, and the deed chain (a cheap LLC purchase followed by a financed retail resale) makes the analyst treat them as acquisitions, not comps. The MLS closed price on Woodbrook differs from the deed by $5,100. Because the engine prices from the deed and treats everything else as evidence, none of this reaches the ARV. A single portal has no second opinion.

What Redfin and Zillow are still good for

  • Photos and remarks at the time of sale, for any house that sold through the MLS. This is the best public record of condition at sale and contractors should keep using it.
  • The asking-price history, which shows how a listing moved. Fulton: $195,900 to $170,500 to a $135,000 sale. Woodbrook: $149,999 to a $155,000 sale after 137 days.
  • Not for value. Neither the asking price nor the estimate is evidence of what a house is worth. The ARV engine prices from closed, arm's-length, deed-recorded sales and nothing else.
